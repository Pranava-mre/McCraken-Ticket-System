# System Overview

Category: General
Keywords: window ticket system, ticket system overview, what is this system, dump ticket, material ticket, what does this application do

Purpose:
The Window Ticket System is McCracken Materials' application for processing trucks at the scale or window, recording incoming and outgoing material loads, printing tickets, searching and correcting past tickets, and running reports for billing and reconciliation.

Procedure:
The system centers on a New Ticket screen used to process trucks live, a Search/Reprint screen used to find and reprint past tickets, an Edit Tickets screen used to correct tickets, a Reports screen used for reconciliation and billing reports, a Daily Report screen showing the Daily Dispatch Dashboard, a Customers screen for adding customers, and Trucks, Edit Trucks, Materials, and RFID areas with differing levels of availability.

Important:
This knowledge base describes procedures and system behavior only. It does not contain live ticket records, live truck load counts, or live customer data. Questions about what happened on a specific date, a specific truck's tickets, or current totals should be answered using the live ticket search tool, not this manual.

# Beginning of Day Setup

Category: Startup

Procedure:

2. Open the Window Ticket application using the approved internal access method.
3. Open three Window Ticket browser tabs.
4. Move one tab to the right monitor.
5. On the right monitor tab, open Reports from the top navigation, then open Daily Report to display the Daily Dispatch Dashboard.
6. On the left monitor, arrange the remaining ticket screens side by side using the operating system window snap controls.

Important:

# Creating a Ticket

Category: Tickets
Keywords: new ticket, create ticket, process truck, check in a truck, process a load, submit ticket, ticket entry, make a ticket

Purpose:
Use this procedure to process a normal live ticket when the truck is already known to the system. New trucks, uncertain materials, special customer handling, and corrections are covered in their own sections.

Procedure:
1. Open the Window Ticket system using the approved internal access method.
2. Open New Ticket.
3. In Truck, type the first characters of the carrier or truck identifier, then choose the correct truck from the suggestions.
4. Choose Direction, either IN or OUT.
5. Choose the correct Material from the list that appears for the selected Direction.
6. In Customer, type the first characters of the paying customer and select the correct result.
7. Enter or select the Job information supplied by the driver. For MREX, Petty, Eagle, and Redcon, select the option containing both the numeric job identifier and the job description.
8. Leave Quantity as 1 and Unit as Load unless an authorized process says otherwise.
9. Review the ticket information, then select Submit Ticket.
10. When the second print screen appears, print two copies for a normal live transaction. Keep the signed copy for records and give the other copy to the driver. Close the remaining print screen manually if required.

Important:
If the truck, material, customer, or job is uncertain, stop and resolve the uncertainty using the relevant procedure before submitting the ticket. Do not guess.

What Happens Next:
Submit Ticket processes the ticket and starts the printing workflow, generating the print screens described above.

Related Topics: New Ticket Screen Overview, Selecting a Truck, Selecting Direction IN or OUT, Selecting a Material, Selecting a Customer, Selecting a Job, Submitting a Ticket and Printing

# Customer Versus Carrier or Broker

Category: Customers
Keywords: broker name, carrier name, customer versus carrier, driver gave broker, who is paying, paying customer versus hauler

Purpose:
This clarifies the difference between the carrier or broker hauling a load and the customer who is actually paying for it, which is a common point of confusion at the window.

Procedure:
When a driver provides a broker or carrier name, determine who is actually paying for the load and enter that as the Customer. A carrier can haul for different customers during the same day, and a driver can be reassigned to another job or customer during the day, so the carrier name alone should not be used to fill in Customer.

Important:
Confirm the paying Customer separately from the carrier or broker identity, especially when a carrier is known to work for more than one customer or job during the day.

# Troubleshooting and Decision Rules

Category: Troubleshooting
Keywords: troubleshooting, what if truck missing, what if wrong material, what if wrong customer, what if wrong job, decision rules, common problems

Purpose:
This provides a quick decision reference for common situations at the window, pointing to the correct detailed procedure for each.

Procedure:
If the truck does not appear, use Truck Not Found and Adding a New Truck. If the incoming material does not match the list, use Material Does Not Match Load and do not guess. If an OUT load is requested, use Direction OUT and Operator Communication to contact the operator before processing. If the driver gives a broker or carrier name instead of the paying customer, use Customer Versus Carrier or Broker. If several job choices appear, use Special Job Selection Rules. If a ticket needs correction, use Editing a Ticket, making only the required correction. If the ticket is from the previous week, do not edit it; use Restrictions on Editing Previous Week Tickets and contact the designated internal person. If a ticket is being entered for the previous day, use Entering a Ticket for a Previous Day. If Edit Trucks or RFID access is needed, see Edit Trucks Status and RFID Status, since the source training marks both as coming soon with no operating procedure.

Important:
Auto print after save is documented as not active or not fully developed, so do not rely on it to print copies automatically; follow Submitting a Ticket and Printing instead.
